## Rethinking ME/CFS

Garett Ridge, 2026

## ABSTRACT

## Preface

Myalgic Encephalomyelitis (ME or ME/CFS) leaves millions of sufferers housebound or bedbound, making it one of the most disabling chronic illnesses ever known. Often triggered by infection, it disrupts multiple physiological systems, with numerous viral epidemic-linked outbreaks documented throughout the twentieth century<sup><a name="cite1a"></a>(#ref1), <a name="cite2a"></a>(#ref2), <a name="cite3a"></a>(#ref3)</sup>. Despite decades of research and political battles over mobilizing resources, the underlying mechanisms remain elusive. As the US Institute of Medicine concluded in 2015, “There is an urgent need for research to elucidate the underlying mechanisms of ME/CFS”<sup><a name="cite4a"></a>(#ref4)</sup>. The goal of this paper is a new hypothesis and model.

Around 10% [cite https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8959090/ ] of people develop ME/CFS within 12 months after a commonly experienced infection, that of Epstein-Barr virus (or "mono"). Most adults are permanent hosts of EBV. That notably includes 100% of people with lupus or multiple sclerosis [cite https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC508514/] [cite https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC508514/ ]. Mono infection is therefore the reason why lupus and MS exist, which reinforces that infectious exposures can leave bodies worse off in complex ways forever.

Patients are categorized under ME/CFS if they develop extended fatigue periods long after stress or overexertion, along with chronically unrestorative sleep. ME/CFS is made up of those obligatory hallmark features, plus a shotgun pattern of comorbidities. Dozens of syndromes are observed more often in the population cluster of ME/CFS patients. Many are seemingly unrelated to each other, while others are hard to differentiate. Chronic illness sufferers come to know long lists of bodily issues whose connectivity seems to defy rational description and verifiability. Yet various effects of the disease leave physical biomarkers proving that the condition is real. New cases continue to mount from EBV outbreaks as well as other viruses that can cause ME/CFS with strikingly similar presentation, most commonly SARS2-nCoV as a component of Long COVID.

Even though ME/CFS patients stand out quantitatively, both as a cluster in aggregate population trends and in microscopic biomarker differences, patients are met with doubt. In the clinical setting there are no definitive tests to confirm ME/CFS, leaving millions without validation that their impairments have a real basis. History complicates the issue; parallel to the reluctance of the US government to recognize the AIDS crisis for its first decade, industry-led campaigns obfuscated the nature and mechanisms of ME/CFS, causing great scandals and changing the course of science. The many academics working to repair the situation face continued obstacles. Today's medical doctors work at a distance from the scientific literature and the competing narratives of chronic illness, often favoring the easier dismissive explanations - having ruled out the acute illnesses that medical schools emphasize. Those who do work in research tend to exclusively work in relative silos of their own medical specializations, and thus no unifying framework has emerged. Thus, part of the onus is on amateur patient researchers to make connections, who are not bound to such restrictions in focus. Patient groups were already instrumental in framing Long COVID as biologically credible, prompting the first academic and NIH recognition of Long COVID [1].  The goal of this paper is a patient-led rethinking of ME/CFS.

The neurological emphasis from the initial outbreak studies was lost starting in the 1970s in favor of psychological, and later, immunological focus. Existing disease models that fixate on measurable chemistry markers have difficulty explaining why so many comorbidities occur, how they relate, or which mechanism within ME/CFS is to blame for each effect. The literature lacks proven causal links between the constellation of chronic pain/fatigue comorbidities. Research writers struggle to frame this constellation as anything besides scattered and disconnected. This common mental model of ME/CFS creates the impression among clinicians that it is mysterious, overcomplex, possibly a bad diagnosis, and of unclear treatment course of action.

To improve that situation, this work aims to demystify ME/CFS. It brings together the existing literature to find causal links between ME/CFS features. The resulting framework indeed clarifies the disease. It is highly plausible, testable, and could outperform the current understanding.

## New Model

Any new hypothesis of ME/CFS must do better than existing models at accoutning for certain mysterious study results. These include, but are not limited to:

A synthesis of literature reveals a novel model for disease, which will be coined here as the "MT disease model," short for "Multi-Tissue Metabolic Overrun disease model". It is a disease of local energy budget overruns. The MT model is consistent with current findings of likely mitochondrial origins of ME/CFS. The key new observation is that every type of tissue in the body reacts differently to a global loss of mitochondrial quality control (MQC). The implications per cell type are numerous and surprisingly predictive of unusual chronic comorbidities. Research has narrowly focused on muscle tissue - likely due to the practical ease of muscle biopsy - at the expense of examining how other cell types react to metabolic overruns and their downstream symptomatology.

Considering different cell types, it is already known that bone cells can react to mitochondrial crisis by developing osteoporosis. For immune cells, their specialized reaction to energy crisis may explain lupus and MS, why they both feature elevated mitochondrial PINK1, and why they are both caused by the same virus. It may also explain the general irreversibility of ME/CFS, by providing a feedback loop that perpetuates mitophagy failure. The reaction of connective tissue cells could explain some hypermobility disorders seen in ME/CFS. The reaction of adrenal epithelial cells explains blood volume abnormalities and the common POTS comorbidity. All of this is in addition to the observed muscle cell apoptosis, which is the least interesting part.

Most importantly to ME/CFS, nerve cells are particularly rich in active mitochondria, moreso than muscle cells, yet this remains unexplored. Nerve cells also don't just get replaced when they have energy crisis, since they have very low turnover. The frequent mitochondrial energy starvation of nerve cells, including of sensory nerves, would naturally degrade all signalling in the nervous system. No frank loss of nerve fibers or even biopsy differences are necessary. The comorbidity Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN), affecting skin sensations and more, does show up on biopsy; it follows more naturally from ME/CFS because of this concept of diffuse peripheral nerve overruns of energy budgets. The sites of the most energy-hungry nerves in the body helps to explain the distribution pattern of frequent problem areas in ME/CFS, including the neck and eye muscles, the larynx and airway, the thoracic outlet and diaphragm, the postural muscles, and the brain.

    Although confirmation in nerve cells might not be as possible or feasible, we might do well to go ahead and assume that they're the problem, for generating other testable hypotheses and treatments.

Local fatigue of nerves would proceed in descending order of their baseline metabolic demand, which disproportionately affects the cranial nerves. This paper will show how signal degradation of fatigueable cranial nerves plausibly leads to many paroxysmal ME/CFS symptoms and crashes previously attributed to the brain - including vertigo, fatigue, postural instability, sensitization, dysautonomia, tinnitus, and migraines. In addition to the connection between IBS and the vagus nerve which is well understood, the downstream effects of other cranial nerves account for previously unexplained comorbidities such as the strongly correlated vocal condition Muscle Tension Dysphonia, or visual impairments such as diplopia. Cranial nerves other than the vagus nerve have been insufficiently considered in ME/CFS, since no mechanism impacting all of them at once was forseen. The energy-based degradation of their signalling could be framed as another expression of SFN, making SFN the driver of most ME/CFS symptomatology when both its overt and unexplored consequences are considered, and increasing priority of finding treatments for it. The aftermath of confusion from historic sabotage of ME/CFS science delayed has that discovery by decades. The current day understanding of mitochondrial damage mechanisms now better demonstrate why such specific neurological effects were discovered in those first viral outbreaks.

This work's result is that the original ME/CFS research was correct to focus on nerve tissue above all, and correct in its inclusion of severe neurological disease cases in outbreaks, including palsies, pareisis, and myoclonus. Nerve cells react to mitochondrial crisis in a way that predicts especially many ME/CFS symptoms and comorbidities. MQC-related energy overruns within nerve cells explain the paroxysmal nature of ME/CFS crashes, and Post-Exertional Malaise (PEM), the hallmark symptom of ME/CFS.

The central role of Mitochondrial Quality Control (MQC) mechanisms in local nerve tissue would help better contextualize Parksinsons disorder and Alzheimers diesease as comorbidities, as well as autism, all of which share highly MQC specific lab abnormalities (elevated PINK1) with ME/CFS, like lupus and MS do.

This paper will be organized as follows. The MT disease framework will be used as a starting assumption of the origins of pathology. To elaborate on this framework, a series of flow chart diagrams will convey all the scientifically plausible causal flows from one ME/CFS phenomenon to another in a cascade of dysfunction. Each part of the flow chart represents a potential research direction for exploring and testing the framework, and for clarifying the overall disease. Next, a collection of existing literature findings will be discussed that already support each causal link. The findings show consistency between the suggested causal links with established features of ME/CFS populations. These discussions will be organized per bodily sub-system.






The first step in making the flow graph was seeding it with certain observed apparent relations in a case study—such as the same muscles being able to activate both neck pain and vocal and airway cut off, combined with a diagnosis of the known comorbidity muscle tension dysphonia.

It was also assumed that there is a bidirectional link between voice and neck pain.

The attempt described in this work begins by assuming one particular causation arrow exists in the graph. It takes as a given that there is a causal link from Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN, commonly detectable in ME/CFS) to the voice and speaking condition called Muscle Tension Dysphonia (MTD). Although voice conditions as subtle as MTD are given relatively little attention in chronic illness cases, there is a high rate of co-morbidity between MTD and ME/CFS that remains totally unexplained, and mostly unknown. Neuropathy is one alternative explanations for why MTD occurs, but other alternatives exist and so that link is not fully understood. Discussions from the SFN side thus never include MTD, or consider it among lists of the more clinically important symptoms of SFN. However, this example of MTD illustrates that a divide exists between the obvious classic symptoms of SFN versus the less intuitive consequences of SFN, which are expected to be widespread. The fact that one of these strange SFN consequences appears often in ME/CFS implies that others might as well. Thus begins a search for other comorbidities of ME/CFS that could instead similarly be demystified as unusual presentations of SFN.

The only truly speculative and unstudied part of this framework is the causal flow from SFN to MTD and from MTD to functional CCI.  This degree of uncertainty is not a unique weakness. existing proposals in prior research for the causes of MTD are no less speculative than this and further don’t account as well for the prevalence of MTD in chronic illness.  Likewise, the existing explanations for CCI in chronic illness (outside of genetic EDS predisposition) are no less speculative, and less satisfying in that they don’t address the concept of mechanical force. Muscle guarding from the larynx and neck  inducing functional CCI solves that, considered along with the more direct effects of SFN upon postural control and feedback. Other nearly universal comorbidities such as tension headache are also more consistent with the guarding theory.

## Methods (Proposed Model)

Although current scientific consensus holds that ME/CFS is fundamentally a disease of microscopic dysfunction (affecting immune, neurovascular, neuroendocrine, and metabolic pathways) these molecular processes do not always clarify how the wide range of day-to-day symptoms arises for patients. The model proposed here does not overrule these molecular origins. Rather, it connects them to larger-scale physical compensations in the neck region, notably including MTD as a hidden central driver and mechanistic bridge between the underlying micro‑pathophysiology and observable clinical realities of ME/CFS.

The prevailing view of ME/CFS today is that it is a multifactorial disease, likely rooted in mitochondrial and immune dysfunctions [cite https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12151296/], while accompanied by a spectrum of comorbidities with variable occurrences in differing patients. Associated conditions and risk factors from CCI to IBS to Long COVID to autism are all conceptualized as a network of interconnected conditions without specifying a unidirectional causal flow between them. In the extreme case that model could be drawn as a totally flat hierarchy like this:
